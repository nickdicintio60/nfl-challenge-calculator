How it works

  • Decision rule. Challenge when p × stake > (1 − p) × timeout + challenge. Break-even confidence is (timeout + challenge) ÷ (stake + timeout). Winning or losing, you spend one of your two challenges, so the challenge's own value sits on both sides. Only the timeout depends on the result.
  • Win probability. Gradient-boosted model on 205,000 plays from nflverse play-by-play (2021–2025), with score, clock, field position, down and distance, timeouts, pregame line, venue and 2nd-half possession. Held-out log loss 0.456, matching nflfastR's own Vegas-adjusted model. Touchdowns assume the extra point and a kickoff to the 30.
  • Challenges. 720 coach's challenges, separated from booth reviews by the play description ("Team challenged the … ruling"). For reversed plays both rulings come from the play text. For upheld plays the alternative ruling is reconstructed from the call type; on reversed plays that reconstruction matches the actual text at r = 0.998.
  • Timeout cost. Fitting timeout value directly from play-by-play gives nonsense, because teams that burn timeouts early are usually teams in trouble. The default instead anchors on Brian Burke's estimate of roughly 3% WP per second-half timeout, applies it to a tied game, halves it in the first half, scales it down as the game gets lopsided (4·WP·(1−WP)), and raises it for your last timeout. Adjust it under Cost assumptions.
  • Value of the challenge itself. Only 10.5% of team-games with a challenge see a second one, so keeping a challenge in reserve is worth about 0.2% WP.
  • Rules built in. No coach's challenges inside two minutes of either half or in overtime, scoring plays and turnovers go to the booth automatically, and you need a timeout to throw the flag.
  • Limits. The leaderboard only sees the flags coaches threw. Plays they should have challenged and didn't are invisible here, and that is where Glassman's argument predicts most of the lost value.

Sources: nflverse play-by-play · Meers, Harvard Sports Analysis Collective (2013, archived) · Glassman, "Challenging" · The Ringer on timeout value (Burke's 3.1% estimate)
